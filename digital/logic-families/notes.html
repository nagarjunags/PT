Chapter 03 · Part 1

Logic Family Taxonomy, Voltage Parameters & Noise Margins

A digital logic family is a standardized collection of integrated digital circuits designed with compatible voltage levels, current drives, and propagation delays. In GATE ECE/EE, problems test internal semiconductor states (cutoff, active, saturation), current sourcing/sinking capabilities, and noise margins.

Standard Voltage Levels & Noise Margins

Four critical voltage thresholds characterize every digital family:

  • $V_{OH,min}$: Minimum output voltage guaranteed when driving a valid HIGH state.
  • $V_{OL,max}$: Maximum output voltage guaranteed when driving a valid LOW state.
  • $V_{IH,min}$: Minimum input voltage recognized by the gate as a valid HIGH state.
  • $V_{IL,max}$: Maximum input voltage recognized by the gate as a valid LOW state.
🎯 Mathematical Definition of Noise Margins

Noise margin quantifies the maximum stray noise voltage that can couple onto an interconnect wire without causing an erroneous logic transition:

$$\text{High-State Noise Margin: } NM_H = V_{OH,min} - V_{IH,min}$$ $$\text{Low-State Noise Margin: } NM_L = V_{IL,max} - V_{OL,max}$$ $$\text{Overall Noise Margin: } NM = \min(NM_H, NM_L)$$

For standard 7400 TTL: $V_{OH,min} = 2.4\text{ V}, V_{IH,min} = 2.0\text{ V} \implies NM_H = 0.4\text{ V}$.
$V_{IL,max} = 0.8\text{ V}, V_{OL,max} = 0.4\text{ V} \implies NM_L = 0.4\text{ V}$. Hence, TTL noise margin is typically $0.4\text{ V}$.

Fan-Out & Current Sinking / Sourcing

Fan-out specifies the maximum number of standard load gate inputs that a single driving gate output can reliably feed without exceeding voltage threshold constraints:

$$\text{High-State Fan-Out} = \left\lfloor \frac{I_{OH,max}}{I_{IH,max}} \right\rfloor \quad (\text{Current Sourcing})$$ $$\text{Low-State Fan-Out} = \left\lfloor \frac{I_{OL,max}}{I_{IL,max}} \right\rfloor \quad (\text{Current Sinking})$$ $$\text{Overall Fan-Out} = \min(\text{High Fan-Out}, \text{Low Fan-Out})$$
Chapter 03 · Part 2

Transistor-Transistor Logic (TTL): Totem-Pole & Open-Collector

Transistor-Transistor Logic (TTL) relies on multi-emitter NPN bipolar junction transistors in the input stage and a totem-pole arrangement in the output stage.

Anatomy of the Standard TTL NAND Gate

  1. Input Stage ($Q_1$): Multi-emitter NPN transistor.
    • If ANY input is LOW ($0.2\text{ V}$): Base-emitter junction of $Q_1$ is forward-biased. Base current flows out of the input terminal ($I_{IL} \approx 1.6\text{ mA}$). The collector of $Q_1$ drops to $\approx 0.4\text{ V}$, starving transistor $Q_2$ of base drive.
    • If ALL inputs are HIGH ($> 2.0\text{ V}$): Base-emitter junctions are reverse-biased. Current from $V_{CC}$ through $R_b$ forward-biases the base-collector junction of $Q_1$ (operating in inverted active mode), driving current into the base of $Q_2$.
  2. Phase-Splitter Stage ($Q_2$): Directs complementary drive signals to the output transistors.
  3. Totem-Pole Output Stage ($Q_3, D, Q_4$):
    • $Q_3$ is the pull-up transistor (acts as emitter follower to source current rapidly).
    • $Q_4$ is the pull-down transistor (saturates to sink current to ground).
    • Role of the Diode ($D$): When output is LOW, $Q_4$ is in saturation ($V_{CE4,sat} \approx 0.2\text{ V}$) and $Q_2$ is in saturation ($V_{BE4,sat} + V_{CE2,sat} \approx 0.7 + 0.2 = 0.9\text{ V}$). The base of $Q_3$ is at $0.9\text{ V}$. Without diode $D$, $V_{BE3} = 0.9 - 0.2 = 0.7\text{ V}$, which would turn $Q_3$ ON! The diode adds an additional $0.7\text{ V}$ threshold requirement ($V_{BE3} + V_D = 1.4\text{ V}$), keeping $Q_3$ completely OFF while $Q_4$ is ON (GATE 1994, Q30)!
⚠️ Open Collector TTL & Wired-AND Logic

Standard totem-pole outputs CANNOT be tied together! If one gate attempts to output HIGH while the other outputs LOW, a near short-circuit path ($V_{CC} \to Q_3 \to D \to Q_4 \to \text{GND}$) is created, drawing excessive current and destroying the ICs.

To safely connect outputs together, use Open-Collector TTL gates with an external pull-up resistor $R_p$. Tying open-collector outputs together naturally implements the Wired-AND function ($Y = Y_1 \cdot Y_2$).

Chapter 03 · Part 3

CMOS Logic Design: PUN/PDN Synthesis, Dynamic Power & Sizing

Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor (CMOS) logic combines PMOS pull-up networks (PUN) and NMOS pull-down networks (PDN). Because one network is always completely OFF in steady state, static power dissipation is virtually zero.

Duality Rules for CMOS Synthesis

For any arbitrary Boolean output $F(A, B, C, \dots)$:

  • Pull-Down Network (PDN): Constructed using NMOS transistors connected between Output and Ground. Realizes $\overline{F}$.
    • AND operation (e.g. $A \cdot B$) $\implies$ NMOS in series.
    • OR operation (e.g. $A + B$) $\implies$ NMOS in parallel.
  • Pull-Up Network (PUN): Constructed using PMOS transistors connected between $V_{DD}$ and Output. Realizes $F$.
    • AND operation $\implies$ PMOS in parallel.
    • OR operation $\implies$ PMOS in series.

Sizing for Equal Noise Margins

In a CMOS inverter, electrons have higher carrier mobility than holes ($\mu_n \approx 2.5 \mu_p$ in silicon). To achieve symmetric voltage transfer characteristics ($V_{inv} = V_{DD}/2$) and equal noise margins ($NM_H = NM_L$):

$$k_n = k_p \implies \mu_n C_{ox} \left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_n = \mu_p C_{ox} \left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_p \implies \left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_p \approx (2 \text{ to } 3) \left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_n$$

If $(W/L)_p = (W/L)_n$, $NM_H \ne NM_L$ (GATE 2022, Q1)!

⚡ Dynamic Power Dissipation in CMOS

Dynamic power dominates during logic transitions due to load capacitor ($C_L$) charging/discharging:

$$P_{dynamic} = C_L V_{DD}^2 f$$

Dynamic power dissipation is directly proportional to switching frequency $f$ and the square of the supply voltage $V_{DD}^2$.

Chapter 03 · Part 4

Emitter-Coupled Logic (ECL): High-Speed Non-Saturated Physics

Emitter-Coupled Logic (ECL) is the fastest of all saturated/unsaturated bipolar logic families because its transistors operate strictly in the active and cutoff regions, never entering saturation!

Physics of ECL Operation

  • Absence of Saturation Delay: In saturated BJT families (like standard TTL), switching from ON to OFF is delayed by base minority carrier storage time ($t_s$). In ECL, transistors are prevented from ever entering saturation, completely eliminating storage delay!
  • Differential Switching: An input transistor pair steers a constant bias current $I_{EE}$ between two branches based on small voltage differences.
  • Small Logic Swing: Logic HIGH $\approx -0.9\text{ V}$, Logic LOW $\approx -1.7\text{ V}$. Logic swing is merely $\Delta V \approx 0.8\text{ V}$. Small voltage swing minimizes charging time of parasitic capacitances ($t = C \Delta V / I$).
  • Negative Power Supply ($-5.2\text{ V}$): Collector resistors are connected to ground ($0\text{ V}$). Any power supply noise on $-5.2\text{ V}$ is rejected as common-mode noise, keeping the output signals referenced clean to ground.
💡 Summary of Logic Family Champions
  • Fastest Family (Least Propagation Delay): ECL ($t_{pd} < 1\text{ ns}$).
  • Lowest Power Dissipation (Static): CMOS ($P_D \approx \text{nW}$).
  • Highest Noise Immunity: HTL (High Threshold Logic, with zener diode) and CMOS ($NM \approx 0.45 V_{DD}$).
  • Highest Packing Density: CMOS.
Chapter 03 · Part 5

Logic Family Interfacing & Pull-Up Level Shifting

Interfacing TTL with CMOS

Directly connecting TTL output to CMOS input often fails due to voltage level mismatch:

  • TTL Output High: $V_{OH,min} = 2.4\text{ V}$.
  • 5V CMOS Input High: $V_{IH,min} = 3.5\text{ V}$ ($0.7 V_{DD}$).
  • Since $2.4\text{ V} < 3.5\text{ V}$, the TTL gate cannot reliably drive the CMOS input into a recognized HIGH state!
  • The Solution: Connect an external pull-up resistor ($R_{pullup} \approx 1 - 3.3\text{ k}\Omega$) between the TTL output and $+5\text{ V}$ $V_{DD}$ rail. When the TTL pull-up transistor turns off, the resistor pulls the line all the way to $+5\text{ V}$, easily satisfying $V_{IH,min}$.

Q1. GATE ECE 2022 1 Mark MSQ / MCQ

Select the correct statement(s) regarding CMOS implementation of NOT gates.

(A) Noise Margin High ($NM_H$) is always equal to Noise Margin Low ($NM_L$), irrespective of device parameters
(B) Dynamic power is zero
(C) Noise Margin High ($NM_H$) and Noise Margin Low ($NM_L$) depend on the ratio of transistor widths $(W/L)_p / (W/L)_n$
(D) Static power consumption is dominated by dynamic switching
🎯 Complete Step-by-Step Derivation & Concept Mapping
  1. Recall that for a CMOS inverter, the switching threshold $V_{M}$ and the unity gain points $V_{IL}, V_{IH}$ depend directly on the transconductance ratio $k_n / k_p = \frac{\mu_n C_{ox} (W/L)_n}{\mu_p C_{ox} (W/L)_p}$.
  2. Only when $k_n = k_p$ (i.e. $(W/L)_p / (W/L)_n \approx \mu_n / \mu_p \approx 2.5$) is the voltage transfer curve symmetric, making $NM_H = NM_L$.
  3. If the widths are changed, the transfer curve shifts, and $NM_H \ne NM_L$.
  4. Therefore, $NM_H$ and $NM_L$ depend fundamentally on the $(W/L)$ ratios (Statement C).
Official ExamGOAL Answer Key: (C)
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert

$NM_H$ is NOT unconditionally equal to $NM_L$; it requires careful matching of $(W/L)_p / (W/L)_n$ to compensate for hole mobility.

Q30. GATE ECE 1994 2 Marks MCQ

In the output stage of a standard TTL, we have a diode between the emitter of the pull-up transistor and the collector of the pull-down transistor. The purpose of this diode is to

(A) protect against negative voltage spikes
(B) prevent both output transistors from conducting simultaneously when output is LOW
(C) increase the fan-out
(D) reduce the propagation delay
🎯 Complete Step-by-Step Derivation & Concept Mapping
  1. When output is LOW, $Q_4$ is in saturation ($V_{CE4,sat} \approx 0.2\text{ V}$).
  2. $Q_2$ is also in saturation, so its collector voltage is $V_{C2} = V_{BE4,sat} + V_{CE2,sat} \approx 0.7 + 0.2 = 0.9\text{ V}$.
  3. This voltage ($0.9\text{ V}$) appears at the base of pull-up transistor $Q_3$.
  4. If the diode were absent, $V_{BE3} = V_{B3} - V_{out} = 0.9\text{ V} - 0.2\text{ V} = 0.7\text{ V}$, which would turn $Q_3$ ON!
  5. Inserting diode $D$ requires $V_{B3} - V_{out} \ge V_{BE3} + V_D = 0.7 + 0.7 = 1.4\text{ V}$ to turn on.
  6. Since $0.9 - 0.2 = 0.7\text{ V} < 1.4\text{ V}$, $Q_3$ stays completely OFF.
  7. This eliminates shoot-through current from $V_{CC}$ to Ground.
Official ExamGOAL Answer Key: (B) prevent both output transistors from conducting simultaneously
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert

Without the diode, both totem-pole transistors would turn on together, causing massive current spikes and thermal destruction.

Q32. GATE ECE 1988 8 Marks Subjective / Design

Implement the function $F = AB + CD$ using two open-collector TTL 2-input NAND gates and an external pull-up resistor.

  4. To obtain uncomplemented $F = AB + CD$, feed through an open-collector inverter stage.
